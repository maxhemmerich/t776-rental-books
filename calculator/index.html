Repairs or capital — the line that trips people up

Does the work restore the property to its previous condition, or does it improve it beyond what it was? If it restores the property, it is a current expense and you deduct it this year, on line 8960 Repairs and maintenance. If it improves the property beyond what it was, it is a capital expense: it joins the cost of the property instead of being deducted now, and it does not go into this year's total.

Current expense — repairs (deduct this year) Capital expense — improvements (add to cost)
Fixing a broken furnace so it works againReplacing the entire furnace with a better model
Patching drywall and repainting the patchA new roof
Replacing a single leaking faucetA kitchen renovation
Repainting between tenantsAdding a bathroom
Patching a roof leakFinishing a basement
Replacing all the windows

Grey areas go to an accountant. Work that is partly repair and partly improvement, a furnace replaced during a larger renovation, a roof patched twice in one year and then replaced — do not guess.
