What to have ready before you file Form T776

A T776 is only as good as what is behind it. Before the form is filled in, the records have to exist - the lease, the rent record, the receipt for every expense you intend to claim, and the log behind every kilometre. 5 Mileage and travel

Driving to the rental to fix a faucet, meet a tenant or buy supplies is a rental expense. CRA's guidance requires that you be able to support the kilometres you claim: a log with the date, the destination, the purpose and the kilometres. No log means no supported deduction.

CRA's prescribed automobile rate for 2026 is $0.72 per kilometre for the first 5,000 kilometres driven for rental (or business) purposes, and $0.66 per kilometre after that. Both figures are CRA's published rates, not ours.

A worked log

Date Destination Purpose Kilometres
2026-01-15 88 Lakeshore Rd, Fernie BC Winter maintenance check 1,240
2026-04-10 88 Lakeshore Rd, Fernie BC Spring repairs and supply run 1,310
2026-07-22 88 Lakeshore Rd, Fernie BC Tenant turnover and cleaning 1,265
2026-10-05 88 Lakeshore Rd, Fernie BC Fall inspection and furnace filter 1,290
Total 5,105

Turning the log into a deduction (line 9281)

First 5,000 km × $0.72 $3,600.00
105 km above the tier × $0.66 $69.30
Total deduction on line 9281 $3,669.30

That is the whole calculation: 5,105 logged kilometres become a $3,669.30 deduction because 5,000 km are rated at $0.72 and the last 105 km at $0.66. Without the log, none of it is claimable.

The log itself is the evidence. Four lines with a date, a destination, a purpose and a number is all CRA expects to see - but it expects to see all four, for every trip you count.

6 The six-year rule

CRA requires records to be kept for six years from the end of the tax year to which they relate. If the records for the 2026 tax year would disappear, they have to stay until the end of 2032. This is CRA's published rule, and it applies whether or not you claimed anything that year.

What to keep

  • Lease agreements and a record of the rent received (bank statements or a rent ledger).
  • Repair and supply receipts, itemized.
  • Vendor invoices, including contractor invoices that show labour and materials separately.
  • Insurance documents for the rental property.
  • Property tax bills.
  • Mortgage interest statements from your lender showing the interest portion.
  • Utility bills for any utilities you pay on the rental.
  • Your mileage log, with date, destination, purpose and kilometres for every trip.

Digital copies are fine. A scanned PDF or a phone photograph kept in a dated folder does the same job as paper, as long as it is readable, complete, and kept for the full six years.

The practical version of this rule: keep everything at the time. The receipt you throw away in March is the one you need in November.

8 Expenses CRA does not allow

Four things regularly appear on drafts of a T776 that should not be there. If you recognise one of yours, take it off before you file.

Not allowed Why, and what to do instead
Mortgage principal Only the interest is a deductible expense (line 8710). The principal repayment builds your equity in the property and is not deducted.
Your own labour If you do the repairs yourself you cannot pay yourself a wage and deduct it. The materials you buy are deductible; your time is not.
Land transfer tax It is a cost of acquiring the property, so it joins the cost base of the property instead of appearing as a current expense. Legal fees on the purchase sit in the same place.
Fines and penalties Parking tickets, municipal fines, late-payment charges and tax penalties are not deductible.

If you are not certain whether something is on this list, that is a question for an accountant. It is a short, cheap question; an assessment later is neither.

9 Before you file - a 12-item checklist

Work down this list with your records open. Each item is something you can check yourself, without advice.

Total rent collected for the year matches your bank deposits and lease records (line 8141).
You have a receipt or invoice for every expense you intend to claim.
Every expense is sitting on the correct T776 line.
Shared home costs are split by a method you can explain and defend (square footage is the usual one).
Costs that relate only to the rental unit are claimed at 100%, not split.
You have separated repairs (current expense) from improvements (capital).
Your mileage log records date, destination, purpose and kilometres for each trip.
You have not claimed mortgage principal, your own labour, land transfer tax or any fine.
Property tax and insurance figures match your municipal and insurer documents.
The personal portion of shared costs is reported at line 9949 and is not deducted.
Your CCA decision is written down, whether you claim capital cost allowance this year or not.
Your net figure at line 9369 is the number you will carry to your T1.

If you cannot tick a box, that is the next thing to fix - not a reason to file anyway.

Frequently asked

What records do I need before I file Form T776?

Keep detailed records of all the rental income you earn and the expenses you incur.

How long do I have to keep T776 records?

CRA requires records to be kept for six years from the end of the tax year to which they relate.

Do I have to send my receipts to the CRA with my return?

Do not send your records with your income tax return.

What happens if an expense cannot be supported?

We may not allow all or part of your expenses if you do not have receipts or other documents to support them.

Can I deduct the mortgage principal, or pay myself for my own repairs?

If you do the repairs yourself you cannot pay yourself a wage and deduct it.
