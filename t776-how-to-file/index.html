1 What T776 is

Form T776 is the Statement of Real Estate Rentals. It is filed with your T1 personal income tax return by landlords who hold rental property in their own name - one person, a couple, or a small group of individuals each filing a T1. Most individual landlords report their rental income and expenses on Form T776; it is the ordinary route for an owner-operator, not an advanced or optional schedule.

You do not file a separate tax return for a rental property. The form gathers the rental income on one side and the related expenses on the other, and produces the net rental income (or loss) that flows into your T1. The rest of this guide walks the lines of that form.

A note on scope

This guide is about an individual who owns rental property in their own name. If the property is held inside a corporation, a trust, or a partnership that files its own return, the reporting is different and is outside what is covered here. Where that is your situation, speak to an accountant.

Everything in this guide that describes a rule describes it as what CRA's published guidance says. It is a reference, not advice. Where a line is genuinely uncertain for your property, the correct move is to ask a qualified accountant - not to guess.

2 The T776 expense line map

These are the expense lines on Form T776, with the code number as it appears on the form. The codes and line names below are used exactly as CRA prints them. The description says, in plain English, what belongs on each line.

Code Line name What belongs on it
8521 Advertising The cost of advertising a rental - online listings, newspaper ads, a yard sign, or a posting fee. Claim it to the extent it relates to the rental.
8690 Insurance Premiums for fire, liability and contents insurance covering the rental property for the period it was available to rent. Your own homeowner policy on your residence stays personal.
8710 Interest and bank charges Mortgage interest (the interest part only, never the principal), interest on money borrowed to buy or improve the rental property, and bank service charges on the account you run the rental through.
8810 Office expenses Small running costs of administering the rental: stationery, postage, printing, and the rental portion of a phone or internet line you use to manage it.
8860 Professional fees (includes legal and accounting fees) Fees paid for the rental itself - legal work on a lease or a tenancy dispute, accounting and bookkeeping fees, appraisals. Legal fees that are part of buying the property are a capital cost, not this line.
8871 Management and administration fees Amounts paid to a property manager, letting agent or rental platform to manage the unit, collect rent or find tenants.
8960 Repairs and maintenance Labour and materials that restore the property to its previous condition - fixing something broken, patching, repainting between tenants. Work that improves the property beyond what it was is capital, not this line.
9060 Salaries, wages and benefits (including employer's contributions) Pay to people who work in the rental operation, together with the employer's share of CPP and EI and any source deductions you remit for them.
9180 Property taxes Municipal and school property tax on the rental property for the period it was rented, including the rental share of a property that is only partly rented.
9200 Travel Travel to and from the rental property for rental purposes where you are not using a vehicle claimed under line 9281 - airfare, taxi or transit. Do not claim both a mileage rate and the actual cost of the same trip.
9220 Utilities Heat, electricity and water for the rental property, including the rental share of utilities you pay on a building you also live in.
9281 Motor vehicle expenses (not including capital cost allowance) Kilometres driven for rental purposes multiplied by CRA's prescribed rate, supported by a mileage log. Capital cost allowance on the vehicle is claimed separately and is not part of this line.
9270 Other expenses Legitimate rental costs that do not fit any line above - for example a bookkeeping software subscription used for the rental, or small one-off costs. If you are unsure whether something belongs here, ask an accountant.

Income and total lines

These are the lines that carry income in and the totals out. They are not expenses, but they are the lines most people read last and misread first.

Code Line name What belongs on it
8141 Gross rents The total rent you collected for the year on the property.
8230 Other income Other rental-related income, such as fees you charge tenants for parking, laundry or extra services.
8299 Total gross rental income Line 8141 plus line 8230 - all the rental income before any expenses.
Amount A Total expenses Your total deductible expenses after the personal portion has been removed. This is the figure you deduct.
9949 Total for personal portion The expenses that relate to your own use of a partly rented property. They are reported here and are not deducted.
9369 Net income (loss) before adjustments Line 8299 minus amount A. This is the rental figure that flows into your T1 personal return.

3 Repairs or capital - the line that trips people up

The most common mistake on Form T776 is putting a capital improvement on a repairs line. CRA's guidance draws the line with one test.

The test

Does the work restore the property to its previous condition, or does it improve it beyond what it was?

  • If it restores the property, it is a current expense. You deduct it this year, on line 8960 Repairs and maintenance.
  • If it improves the property beyond what it was, it is a capital expense. It joins the cost of the property instead of being deducted now. Capital costs are generally written off over time through capital cost allowance, which is not the same thing as a repair.
Current expense - repairs (deduct this year) Capital expense - improvements (add to cost)
• Fixing a broken furnace so it works again • Replacing the entire furnace with a better model
• Patching drywall and repainting the patch • A new roof
• Replacing a single leaking faucet • A kitchen renovation
• Repainting between tenants • Adding a bathroom
• Patching a roof leak • Finishing a basement
• Replacing all the windows

Grey areas go to an accountant. Work that is partly repair and partly improvement, a furnace replaced during a larger renovation, a roof patched twice in one year and then replaced - these are the cases where a wrong call is expensive. Do not guess. Ask a qualified accountant before you file, and keep their answer with your records.

7 Working the form in order

Form T776 reads top to bottom in a fixed order, and the sensible way to approach it is to fill it in that same order. This is the sequence, using the line numbers from the map in section 2.

  1. Income first. Enter the rent you collected on 8141 and any other rental income on 8230. Added together they give 8299, total gross rental income.
  2. Then the expenses. Put each cost on its own line from the map. Costs that relate only to the rental unit go in at 100%.
  3. Then the split. For costs shared with your own home, work out the rental share (section 4) and claim only that share on the expense line. The personal share is reported at 9949 and is not deducted.
  4. Then add up what you are claiming. The subtotal of the expense lines you are claiming is amount A, total expenses.
  5. Then subtract. 8299 minus amount A gives 9369, net income (loss) before adjustments. That is the figure that flows into your T1.
  6. Then keep everything. The receipts and the log behind every figure stay for six years (section 6).

Two habits make the rest of it easy. Fill the form from the records, not from memory. And if a figure cannot be traced to a receipt or a log line, treat it as unproven until it can.
